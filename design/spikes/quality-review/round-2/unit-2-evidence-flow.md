## Findings (none blocking; three correctness, one mechanical)

### F1 (correctness, probe-backed): one shared producer-stops timeout starves every later producer's stop(); a starved Prober leaks and probes forever

`EvidenceSession.finalize` runs all registered producer stops sequentially inside one `asyncio.timeout(PRODUCER_STOPS_SHARE_S=6.0)` (`src/inspect_ranges/_provider/evidence.py:250-258`). When the FIRST stop outlives the share, the timeout cancels the loop and every LATER producer's `stop()` is never invoked. Registration order is fixed by `_challenges/setup.py:314-320`: TelemetryPoller first, Prober second. A telemetry stop legitimately exceeds 6 s whenever a guest is wedged at teardown (an in-flight poll holds the channel budget, `POLL_COMMAND_MS=10s`/`POLL_UNTIMED_BOUND_MS=30s`, and `TelemetryPoller.stop` waits up to 20 s before cancelling; `virsh suspend` is a battery-demonstrated poll-wedge). The poller itself winds down safely (its `stop()` sets `_stopping` before any await), but the Prober's `_stopping` is never set and its tasks are never cancelled (`_challenges/prober.py:120-128, 181-187`): each target keeps spawning the probe client (`docker compose exec`) every 2 s and force-flushing `down` records into the now-sealed writer (refused, exception-logged) for the remaining life of the driver process, which in an Inspect eval spans many samples. The round-1 pin (`tests/test_evidence_session.py:213`, `test_wedged_producer_stop_cannot_starve_the_seal`) covers only seal starvation with a single producer; nothing covers the second producer.

Probe (probe_r2u2.py, `test_probe_a_starved_producer_keeps_probing`): real `Prober` with a stub client at 0.05 s cadence registered after a 5 s wedged stop; `PRODUCER_STOPS_SHARE_S` patched to 0.2 s; `finalize()` then 0.5 s of settling. Observed:

```
PROBE A: prober task alive after finalize=True; client invocations finalize->+0.5s: 4 -> 14
```

The record seals correctly (`incomplete`); the defect is the unbounded leaked producer (task + subprocess churn + log spam), the sibling of round-1 B19 one layer up. Fix shape is adjudication's call (cancel/flag all producers before awaiting any, or per-producer shares); regression test implied.

### F2 (correctness, probe-backed): a cancelled finalize is latched as finalized, so the retried teardown seals nothing and then releases the lease, leaving a permanently unsealed, unreferenced record with an empty binding

`finalize` sets `self._finalized = True` before doing any work (`evidence.py:244-247`) and never resets it on cancellation. External cancellation (the second-ctrl-C family, during normal `sample_cleanup` teardown) propagates out of any of finalize's three phases as `CancelledError` (not caught; `asyncio.timeout` re-raises foreign cancellations). `_destroy` aborts before `down`. When `task_cleanup` retries `_destroy` (the handle stays in the registry, `provider.py:546`), `finalize()` is a no-op, `down` succeeds, `write_binding()` writes `seals={}`, and the CID and host leases are released (`provider.py:535-549`). With the lease gone, neither the reaper nor `cli_cleanup` can ever reap-seal it. This is round-1 A6's exact end state (seal-less binding plus released retry anchor) reached through the cancellation arm instead of the budget arm. It is strictly worse than driver death: a dead driver's lease survives and the reaper seals `reaped`; the successful retry destroys the only pointer. Distinct from the recorded second-cancellation residual, which is the mid-acquisition owner-record case.

Probe (probe_r2u2.py, `test_probe_b_cancelled_finalize_never_retries_the_seal`): open a session, append one driver chunk, cancel `finalize()` while it awaits a blocking producer stop, then run the retry sequence (finalize, write_binding). Observed:

```
PROBE B: binding seals={}; driver sealed=None; unreferenced=['chunks/driver/session/000000.bin']
```

Note the aggravation: because the cancelled run never reached a part cut and the no-op retry does not either, even the already-delivered chunks end as unreferenced residue, so `verify` sees no driver writer at all. The applier's shutdown arm checkpoints for exactly this reason; the driver's cancelled-finalize path has no analog.

### F3 (correctness, traced arithmetic): the applier's shutdown flush wraps `_stop_evidence` in a 5 s outer timeout whose inner bounds sum to ~32 s, so the applier-shutdown gap + checkpoint arm it exists for is routinely cancelled

`AspApplier.aclose` (`src/inspect_ranges/_host/applier.py:222-236`) wraps `self._stop_evidence(lease_id, seal=False)` in `asyncio.timeout(5.0)`. Inside, `streamer.stop(stop_timeout_s=6.0, harvest_timeout_s=10.0)` is internally bounded at up to ~22 s (poll-cycle await 6 + sidecar stop wait 6 + harvest 10; `_evidence/capture.py:303-361`), and the `applier-shutdown` gaps + `checkpoint()` arm then runs under a further 10 s reserve (`applier.py:694-708`). The harvest bound was deliberately sized at 10 s "for a REAL teardown tail", which alone cannot fit inside the 5 s outer cap. Even the clean path (sidecar stop ~1-2 s via a 0.5 s supervisor tick plus per-segment tcpdump termination, then compose-cp harvest of the pcap tail) plausibly exceeds 5 s on any sample with real capture volume. When the outer timeout fires, the cancellation lands mid-stop and the gap/checkpoint arm never runs: no `applier-shutdown` gap records, buffered manifest records uncommitted, delivered tail chunks left unreferenced. That contradicts both the stated contract of the arm itself (the shutdown gaps "must land in a DELIVERED manifest part or the unsealed record shows nothing", `applier.py:699-701`) and the A6 review lesson applied verbatim two screens below ("no outer timeout, which would cancel its own gap-recording handlers mid-write", `applier.py:682-684`). The existing pin (`tests/test_host_applier.py:731`) exercises only a fast LocalTap that finishes well under 5 s. Consequence is bounded (the record is already destined for an unsealed reap-seal, honest up to its last delivered part) but the loss goes unmarked, which is the one forbidden shape. Classic round-1 cross-layer envelope mismatch, second instance of the A6 family.

### F4 (mechanical): `task_cleanup(cleanup=False)` comment describes only the uds arm

`provider.py:588-592`: "an applier-side writer keeps capturing and the reaper reap-seals it after lease expiry" is true only for the `uds:` topology. In the local topology the same `finalize()` call stops the sidecar and seals the host writer in this process; nothing keeps capturing and the reaper has nothing to seal. Behavior is fine (deliberate, per the adjacent "honest to the abandonment point"); the comment misstates one of the two arms it covers.

## Considered and declined

1. Chunk/part key zero-padding (`%06d`) breaks lexicographic ordering past seq 999,999: unreachable volumes (4 GB per-writer cap over 10 MB rotation chunks is ~410 pcap chunks; telemetry at 2 s cadence over 8 h is ~14k).
2. Positional span alignment in `evidenceread.stream_chunks_with_spans` misassigns if a foreign object occupies a gapped sequence slot (write-once-conflict): requires store write access the threat model denies the agent; the honest conflict arm is the recorded S3 409/412 posture.
3. `corroborate`'s `covered_until` uses ship-time chunk spans, which overstate packet-capture coverage: the contradiction arm is reachable only for a `corroborated=True` latch whose flow is absent from delivered chunks, which an honest check cannot produce (the check read the same delivered chunks, and corroborate's fresh fetch is a superset); the boundary is discussed in the module docstring.
4. pcap chunk spans are poll-cycle spans, not packet intervals: recorded ("window edges are decided at batch granularity", evidenceread docstring and goal-type docstrings).
5. Binding lost when the driver dies between seal and a successful down: inherent to driver death; the INFO mirror and the reaper's no-binding posture are recorded residuals. (F2 is distinct: no death, retry succeeds, anchor destroyed.)
6. Local-topology `cleanup=False` seals the host capture `complete` and stops capture while the range stays up: deliberate per the in-code comment; scoring has concluded and nothing consumes post-abandonment capture. (The comment itself is F4.)
7. `admission_memory_mb` charges two buffer caps in the local topology even when the plan renders no capture (the host writer never opens): one-buffer over-charge, conservative direction, admission is a heuristic.
8. `S3Store` maps GET `AccessDenied` to `FileNotFoundError`, so a mis-scoped scoring credential reads as absent evidence: documented in-code, warn-once logged, and the alternative (tracebacks instead of first-break reports) is worse; goal types go indeterminate, never falsely pass.
9. Finalize worst case (~38 s) exceeds the nominal `FINALIZE_BUDGET_S` 30 s: recorded explicitly in the `PRODUCER_STOPS_SHARE_S` docstring as the deliberate trade (only the seal reserve is guaranteed).
10. `_in_window_chunks` span-shape handling: recorded residual, not re-reported; T7 (host-seal fallback binding test) verified still open and not re-reported.
11. Streamer stderr re-ship after a size regression duplicates shipped bytes: duplication, never loss, on a diagnostics stream.
12. Doctor's derived-sink arm evaluates `INSPECT_LOG_DIR` while the runtime derives from the active sample's `log_location`: closest host-side approximation, and both arms share the A11 lockstep predicate `valid_sink_location`.
13. `S3Store._denied_warned` class-default-plus-instance-shadow: intentional warn-once idiom.
14. `sealer._replay_for_seal` initializes each `StreamSummary.chain` from the first record then overwrites it in a final loop: redundant initialization, no drift risk worth a finding.

## Quality

The evidence flow reads as a coherent, honestly-scoped system. The format core (chaining, write-once discipline, seal semantics, the verifier's first-break reporting) is tight, and the round-1 fixes hold where I crossed them: B12's unified FIFO is enforced at every gap entry point, B13's claim protocol closes the capture-cap race from both sides, B14's `bounded_put` posture composes correctly with the SDK-transparent S3 client, A3's final-line-only cursor stripping is correctly reasoned against adversarial output, A4's span-less-tail inclusion is threaded consistently from writer batching through evidenceread to the goal types, and A7's reap-seal is present on all three recovery paths (reaper, cli_cleanup, applier teardown). What remains defective clusters exactly where round 1's taxonomy predicted: teardown-time budget composition across layers. All three correctness findings are instances of the A6 family (a shared timeout starving the arm after the first slow member; an idempotency latch that treats cancellation as completion; an outer bound smaller than the inner bounds it wraps), in the fix-round's own newest code. Nothing I found corrupts scoring or forges evidence; the failure direction is consistently honest (unsealed, unreferenced, incomplete) except that F2 destroys the retry anchor and F3 skips the loss markers. This unit found less than round 1, as predicted, and the found set is narrow enough to say the flow's core is sound.
